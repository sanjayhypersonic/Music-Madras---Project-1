| R-21 | **Recording-level holds (implemented 2026-10-24, committed as a local checkpoint).** A hold is four nullable columns on `recordings` (reason RIGHTS/COPYRIGHT/PRIVACY/OTHER, note, time, who; a CHECK keeps them all-or-none), imposed and lifted on Review & Publish by anyone who may publish (`RECORDING_PUBLISH`, checked again on the server, like a video hold), each with a `RECORDING_HOLD_SET` / `RECORDING_HOLD_LIFTED` audit entry in the same transaction under the per-Recording edit lock. It changes no status, pointer or revision, and lifting it publishes nothing. **Enforced in the canonical readers:** `isRecordingPubliclyVisible` (published AND not held) and its SQL twins `RECORDING_NOT_HELD_WHERE` / `PUBLIC_RECORDING_WHERE` are used by the Recording page and lookup, archive search (rows and total), the sitemap, related content on every entity page, collections, learning guides, Occasion pages and the video page's link to its Recording; `PUBLISHED_REVISION_WHERE` is hold-aware, so a held Recording also stops lifting its performance, programme, venue, participants, work and composer into discovery (they stay discoverable through any other public content), exactly as unpublishing does. A source-level test fails if a public file goes back to the publication-only predicate. **Decisions and assumptions:** (1) a hold is independent of video holds in both directions; the video's own page is unaffected, but its link to a held Recording is removed. (2) A held Recording simply 404s publicly, like an unpublished one; there is no public `unavailable` notice. (3) Publication-state consumers inside Studio (editorial queues, content suggestions, the AI context) keep the publication-only meaning of `isRecordingPublished` on purpose. (4) One active hold per Recording; history is the audit log only (no hold-history table), no expiry or review date, no notification. (5) A Recording can be held in any state, including draft or archived; the hold then waits. **Authorization model (reviewed 2026-10-24):** impose and lift both require `RECORDING_PUBLISH`, checked on the server in the action before the repository is called (a forged POST is refused the same way; refused attempts write nothing, not even an audit entry); the matrix is pinned for all six roles by a test. A hold's NOTE (a rights, copyright or privacy matter) is readable only by those roles: the visibility-workspace loader drops it at the source unless told otherwise, and the Review page withholds it; everyone else still sees that the recording is held and the kind of hold. The audit log (`AUDIT_VIEW`) keeps the note with the actor and time. Known small limits: lifting takes no expected-state token, so if a hold is lifted and a different one imposed between a page load and a click, the click lifts the newer hold (the audit entry names who and when); no notification is raised when a hold is imposed or lifted (who may impose is settled: D-08). **Limits:** (the Occasion workspace's wording for a performance or programme whose only published recording is held was corrected 2026-10-24: it now says the recording is under a hold, from the canonical rules, and keeps `no published recording` for the genuinely unpublished case); `PostgresCatalogueSearchProvider` (`modules/db/search`) is unused and has no publication or hold check, so it must not be wired to a public surface without both; caching in front of the application is covered by R-22 | Medium | before launch (verify on a real host) | recordingHoldRepository, publicEligibility |
| R-22 | **Public cache safety for Recording holds (verified locally 2026-10-24; hosting layer still unverified).** *Verified on a production build* (`next build --webpack` + `next start`, copy outside OneDrive, test database only, no restart or invalidation between steps): the application caches nothing on the public Museum. A fully connected Recording was probed twice per URL, then a hold was imposed through the real repository, probed again, lifted, probed again. The Recording page and the work, performance, programme, venue, composer and Occasion pages went 200 → 404 → 200 on the very next request; the collection page, the video page's link to the Recording, archive search and the sitemap stopped and resumed mentioning it the same way. Pages answer `private, no-cache, no-store` with no `x-nextjs-cache` header, the sitemap `public, max-age=0, must-revalidate`; the prerender manifest lists no Museum route and no ISR entry; there are no `revalidate` / `generateStaticParams` / `unstable_cache` / `use cache` constructs, no middleware, and no process-level cache in the readers. So no invalidation exists or is needed today, and every reader enforces the hold-aware rules on every request. `tests/unit/publicCacheSafety.test.ts` guards this: it fails if any caching construct appears under a public route or reader, and (if one ever does) it requires the hold actions to revalidate public paths. **Launch-critical risks that remain:** (1) *ADR-0001 §10 plans SSG + on-demand ISR on Vercel for these routes*; the moment that is implemented, every visibility change must revalidate affected paths: Recording publish, unpublish, archive, restore and hold, video hold, approval and archival, collection, guide and Occasion changes. None of them does today, because nothing is cached; the guard test will stop the change until it is done. (2) *Not verified: the real host.* Nothing here shows what Vercel's edge, or any CDN placed in front, does with `public, max-age=0, must-revalidate` on the sitemap (a CDN that ignores `max-age=0` would serve a stale sitemap that still lists a held Recording's URL; the URL itself would 404). Before launch: on the real host, hold a published test Recording and confirm its page, search result and sitemap entry go within seconds, then lift it and confirm they return, including through any CDN. (3) Browsers keep no copy (`no-store`), but the Next client router cache and prefetching on a real device were not exercised. Local procedure notes: the app refuses to start in production unless `PUBLIC_SITE_URL` and `AUTH_URL` are non-localhost `https://` URLs, and the project folder sits in OneDrive, which locks `.next` during a build | **High (launch gate: real-host verification)** | before launch | publicCacheSafety.test.ts, ADR-0001 §10 |
